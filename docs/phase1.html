Glypican-3 in cancer: expression, survival and protein structure

Glypican-3 (GPC3) is a cell-surface protein that is almost silent in adult tissues but switched back on in liver cancer, which makes it a target for diagnosis, antibodies and CAR-T therapy. In this diploma project we asked where GPC3 is over- or under-expressed across cancers, whether it predicts survival in liver cancer, which proteins it works with, and what its structure looks like.

GPC3 is strongly over-expressed in liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), confirmed with two tools and my own statistics: a clear diagnostic marker.

It does not separate patients by survival in LIHC, either overall or disease-free, in GEPIA2 or KM Plotter: a weak prognostic marker on its own.

Its network points to Wnt signalling, and its structure is a folded central domain between flexible ends.

Why GPC3?

GPC3 is a heparan sulfate proteoglycan held on the outside of the cell membrane by a GPI anchor. During development it helps control Wnt, Hedgehog and FGF signalling; in most adult tissues it is barely expressed. In hepatocellular carcinoma it is reactivated in a large share of tumours, and because it sits on the cell surface it is used in immunohistochemistry and tested as a target for antibodies and CAR-T cells. Its pattern in other cancers, and whether it predicts how patients do, was much less clear.

Aims

  1. Compare GPC3 expression between tumour and normal tissue across cancer types.
  2. Test whether GPC3 predicts survival (overall and disease-free) in liver cancer.
  3. Map its protein–protein interactions and pathways.
  4. Analyse the structure of the human GPC3 protein.

Workflow

  • STRING at high confidence (0.9), functional enrichment by FDR
  • KEGG pathway confirmation
  • GEPIA and the Human Protein Atlas for expression across organs and cancers

Checking LIHC myself in Python

I downloaded TCGA-LIHC RNA-seq from UCSC Xena, split samples by barcode (tumour -01, normal -11) and compared them with Welch's t-test, which allows unequal group sizes and variances. GPC3 was far higher in the 371 tumours (median 12.67) than in the 50 normal livers (median 6.75), p < 0.0001, matching GEPIA2. Results held when the expression cut-off and significance level were changed.

Partners and pathways

23 interaction partnersSTRING at high confidence (0.9); the most significant enriched process was canonical Wnt signalling.
Proteoglycans in cancerKEGG places GPC3 in the proteoglycans-in-cancer pathway, acting on Wnt and Hedgehog signalling.
Disease linksSTRING disease associations include hepatoblastoma, hepatoid adenocarcinoma and endodermal sinus tumours.
Across organsGEPIA and the Human Protein Atlas both show the highest tumour expression, and the biggest jump, in liver cancer.

The GPC3 protein

580 amino acids: a signal peptide at the start, one large glypican domain, and a C-terminal signal for the GPI anchor that ties it to the membrane.

Conserved coreHuman, chimpanzee and mouse GPC3 are most alike across the central domain; the two ends vary more.
Mixed foldPSIPRED predicts a mainly helical core with short strands, as expected for glypicans.
Flexible endsIUPred2A scores the signal peptide and the C-terminal region as disordered, and AlphaFold has low confidence there too.
GlycosylationPredicted N-glycosylation sites and surface loops where the heparan sulfate chains attach.

What I learned

Data and statistics

  • Working with TCGA and GTEx RNA-seq through UCSC Xena and GEPIA2
  • Testing tumour vs normal in Python with the right test for unequal groups
  • Survival analysis: Kaplan–Meier, hazard ratios and their limits
  • Checking a result with a second tool before believing it

Proteins and pathways

  • Interaction networks and enrichment in STRING, pathways in KEGG
  • Sequence features, alignment and conservation
  • Secondary structure, disorder and structural models (PDB, AlphaFold)
  • Turning many tools' outputs into one biological story

What came next

Phase 2 rebuilds this as one reproducible Python pipeline on public data: all 33 TCGA cancers, diagnostic accuracy with confidence intervals, an adjusted survival model, copy number and methylation, the genes that move with GPC3, and the AlphaFold structure in 3D.
